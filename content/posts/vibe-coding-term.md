“Vibe coding” is an interesting phrase, but it doesn’t quite capture what experienced engineers are actually doing with AI.

There is very little “vibe” involved when you’re building production systems.

In practice, this looks much closer to AI-assisted coding.

When used well, AI is not driving the process it is being guided:

+ Problems are still carefully decomposed 
+ Trade-offs are still explicitly considered 
+ Outputs are still validated against real system constraints 

The leverage comes from speed, not from outsourcing thinking.

From a leadership perspective, this distinction matters.

If teams internalize this as “vibe coding,” you get inconsistency. Different engineers, different standards, unpredictable outcomes.

If it is understood as AI-assisted engineering, you can start to build around it:
clear workflows, review patterns and expectations of rigor.

AI does not reduce the need for strong engineering fundamentals.

If anything, it makes the gap between strong and weak engineering practices more visible.
